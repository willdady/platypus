# Users & access

Sign-in is **email and password**. That is the whole list: there is no SSO, no
OIDC, no SAML, no social login, and no configuration that adds any of them. If an
identity-provider requirement is a condition of adopting Platypus, it is not met
today.

Sessions are cookies, signed with `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET` and valid for **7 days**.
Rotating that secret signs everybody out.

## Who can create an account

That is the Operator's call, made with one backend variable:
`REQUIRE_INVITATION_TO_SIGN_UP`. It is **off by default**, so upgrading changes
nothing until you set it.

### Open sign-up (the default)

Self-serve sign-up is **open**. The sign-in page links to **Sign up**, the form
takes a name, an email and a password, and the account exists immediately —
**email verification is off**, so no address is ever confirmed.

<Callout type="warning">
  Put Platypus on the public internet with sign-up open and strangers will be
  able to register accounts. Either require an invitation (next section) or
  treat network reachability as the access control: keep the frontend behind a
  VPN, an SSO-aware reverse proxy, or an IP allowlist if the set of people who
  may hold an account is not "anyone who can route to it".
</Callout>

The mitigating half, worth knowing before you panic:

A self-registered account has **no Organization and no Workspace**. Signing in
gets them an empty shell — nothing to read, nothing to run, no model, no Chats.
Only a **Super Admin** can create an Organization, and only an **Org Admin** can
invite someone into one. So open sign-up leaks the ability to hold an account, not
the ability to reach anything in your deployment.

Registered accounts still accumulate rows in your database, and a registered
address is enough to accept an invitation later, so the exposure is worth closing
rather than shrugging at.

### Invitation-only sign-up

Set `REQUIRE_INVITATION_TO_SIGN_UP=true` on the backend and restart it. From then
on an account comes into existence in exactly one way: somebody redeems an
[invitation link](#inviting-someone-in).

What changes:

- The public sign-up endpoint is closed. The sign-in page no longer shows
  **Sign up**, and the sign-up page, if someone opens it directly, says that
  sign-up is by invitation and offers no form.
- Nothing else. Sign-in, **Change password**, the password reset on
  **Settings → Users**, and first-boot seeding of the admin all work as before.

What the invitee experiences is the **Not signed in** path below: they open the
link, choose their own name and password, and land in their new Workspace.
Sign-up is still self-service — nobody sets a password for them, and no
password passes through an administrator's hands. Only the entrance changed.

<Callout type="warning">
  **The link is the credential.** Whoever holds an invitation link can redeem it
  and become the account it names, so send it over a channel you would trust
  with a password. If a link reaches the wrong person there is no revoke
  button: delete the invitation — the trash icon at the end of its row in
  **Organization settings → Invitations**, then **Delete** — and send a fresh
  one. Expiry bounds the window either way.
</Callout>

Rate limiting for sign-in and invitation links belongs at the reverse proxy —
see [Put a rate limiter in front](/self-hosting/production#put-a-rate-limiter-in-front).

## Inviting someone in

<Steps>

### Send the invitation

**Organization settings → Invitations**. Fill in:

- **Email** — the address of the person joining. Whoever redeems the link
  gets an account on this exact address; capitalisation does not matter:
  `Bob@Example.com` and `bob@example.com` are the same person here.
- **Workspace name** _(optional)_ — a Workspace provisioned for them on
  acceptance. Left blank, it is named after them.
- **Blueprints** _(optional)_ — one or more
  [Blueprints](/administering/shared-resources) to apply to that Workspace, in the
  order you arrange them.

Click **Send invitation**.

### Copy the link

Every pending invitation in the table has a copy button at the end of its row,
shown as a copy icon with the tooltip **Copy invitation link**. Click it to
copy a `/invite/...` link that stays good for as long as the invitation is
pending and unexpired — the same link works whether the recipient has an account
yet or not.

### Tell them yourself

Nothing is emailed. See the warning below.

### They open the link

What they see depends on whether they're signed in, and as whom:

- **Not signed in** — a short form: their name and a password of at least 8
  characters. The email is fixed to the invited address and can't be changed.
  Submitting it creates the account and joins the Organization in one step;
  there is no separate sign-up first. They land in the Workspace that was
  provisioned for them.
- **Have an account but are signed out** — they choose **Already have an
  account? Sign in** on the invitation page, enter their existing password, and
  then click **Accept invitation**. They stay on the page throughout.
- **Already signed in as the invited address** — a single **Accept
  invitation** button. It lands them in the Workspace that was provisioned for
  them, even if they belong to other Organizations too.
- **Signed in as a different address** — refused, with an explanation and a
  **Sign out** button. The invitation is untouched and still pending; they
  can sign out and sign in with the invited account on the same page.

Either of the first two provisions the Workspace, with any Blueprints applied.

</Steps>

<Callout type="warning">
  **Platypus sends no email. Ever.** There is no SMTP configuration, no mail
  transport, and no delivery to wait for. "Send invitation" writes a row; it
  does not notify anybody. Copy the link yourself and send it over whatever
  channel you already use. Watching an inbox for a Platypus invitation is
  watching an inbox nothing will arrive in.
</Callout>

### Expiry

An invitation is valid for `INVITATION_EXPIRY_DAYS` days, **7** by default. The
Invitations table shows each one's **Status** and **Expires** date, and an expired
invitation cannot be accepted — send a new one.

## Roles

Three levels of authority, each bounded by the one above:

| Role                | Is                                                        | Gets it by                                                 |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Super Admin**     | The platform account. Bypasses all in-app authorization   | Seeded on first boot from `ADMIN_EMAIL` / `ADMIN_PASSWORD` |
| **Org Admin**       | Holds the Organization's credentials and Shared resources | An existing Org Admin sets their **Org Role** to `admin`   |
| **Workspace Owner** | Owns one Workspace and everything composed inside it      | Assigned when the Workspace is created                     |

An Org Admin changes roles from **Organization settings → Members**, using a
row's **Edit org role**, and can remove somebody with **Remove from Org**. A
**Super Admin** row shows no actions there — the platform account is not an Org
Admin's to manage.

## The platform user-management screen

A Super Admin gets one extra screen: **Settings → Users**, titled **User
Management**. It lists every account on the deployment with its role
(**Super Admin** or **User**), its status (**Verified**, **Unverified** or
**Banned**), and when it was created.

Expect almost everyone to read **Unverified**. Only the admin seeded on first boot
is marked verified; because email verification is off there is no flow that ever
verifies anybody else. It is not a problem to fix — nothing is gated on it.

Two actions per row: **Change password** and **Delete**.

This is the screen to use when somebody has locked themselves out, or when a
self-registered account should not exist. It is also the recovery path referred to
elsewhere as "the in-app user management".

<Callout type="info">
  Neither action is offered on your own row, or on another **Super Admin**'s.
  You cannot delete yourself by accident, and you cannot demote or lock out the
  other platform admin from here — that needs database access.
</Callout>
